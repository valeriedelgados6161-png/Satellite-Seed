Bridging NASA Data with the Ground Truth of Azuero

Satellite Seed turns NASA climate records into crop-rotation options for smallholder farmers in Panama’s Arco Seco, then checks every option against what farmers actually live in the field.

See how it works Read the method

In the Arco Seco, the dry season shapes every planting

Panama’s Arco Seco, which includes areas of Herrera, Los Santos and Coclé, is recognized in national environmental material as a region with low annual precipitation and recurring water-deficit challenges. A University of Technology of Panama research paper also describes the Dry Arc as having the country’s lowest precipitation. These conditions make rainfall timing relevant to agricultural decisions. MiAMBIENTE – LADA · UTP / APANAC research (2021).

Meanwhile, NASA POWER provides historical meteorological data that can be queried by geographic coordinates. These are gridded or model-based datasets, not measurements taken on every individual farm plot; their spatial resolution and uncertainty must be shown clearly. The daily API documentation explains the available time-series service, while NASA’s methodology describes the underlying data sources. NASA POWER Daily API · NASA POWER methodology.

The data exists. It just does not reach the people who need it most.

What published evidence tells us. MiAMBIENTE identifies the Arco Seco as Panama’s lowest-precipitation region, with an average near 1,000 mm per year and recurring water-deficit periods that can last up to seven months. This is regional context—not a statement from an interviewed farmer and not a measurement for every plot. Source: MiAMBIENTE – LADA.

From a pin on the map to a clear next step

A farmer should not need to understand a satellite to benefit from one. Satellite Seed does the translation.

  1. Mark the plot

    The farmer drops a pin on the map and says what they grow now and what matters most: saving water, income, or soil health.

  2. Pull NASA’s climate record

    The coordinates are sent to NASA POWER, which returns historical gridded meteorological estimates for the selected location. The result represents the dataset’s grid cell or product resolution—not a rain gauge located on the exact plot. The prototype records the parameter, product, date range, units and resolution used.

  3. Add what the farmer knows

    When the rains really started last year, which plots dry out first. Field knowledge sits next to the satellite record.

  4. See two or three options

    Each rotation shows the rule behind it and a confidence label, so the farmer can agree, adjust, or ignore it.

Climate evidence, checked against local records

Regional climate records and local historical measurements provide useful context, but they are not interchangeable. Satellite Seed will eventually compare NASA POWER estimates for a selected coordinate with local station data and farmers’ accounts. The published figures shown here are evidence available now; they are not presented as completed field validation.

How we check

  • Interviews with local producers, with their consent, using the same questions for each.
  • Their answers are compared with the NASA record for the same place and period.
  • Every figure is labeled by origin: NASA dataset/product, farmer report, official local source, or assumption. NASA POWER also publishes methodology and validation information; users should not interpret global validation statistics as a local validation of an individual Azuero plot.
  • Rotation rules are reviewed by an agronomist or experienced producer before use. FAO describes crop rotation as a practice that can support soil structure, fertility, and pest and weed management; the suitable sequence still depends on local crops, soils, water and farm conditions.

Built to be simple on purpose

This is a lean prototype made for the 48 hours of the hackathon. Here is exactly what it is and what it is not.

Prototype
Python application built during NASA Space Apps 2026.
NASA data
NASA POWER daily meteorological data, accessed through its public API. The current five-year demonstration uses the Agua Buena corregimiento reference coordinate (7.8375° N, 80.4003° W), not a farmer’s individual plot. The selected parameters, units, date range and product are documented in the live table.
Interface
Mobile-first and written in plain language. Farmers get Spanish; reviewers get English summaries.
Transparency
Rules are visible, and every figure shows where it came from.
Limits
It supports a farmer’s decision. It does not replace an agronomist, and it has not yet been tested at scale.

Where Satellite Seed goes next

A rotation choice is one farm-level decision. With appropriate local validation, tools that help producers interpret climate information may contribute to more informed planning. The project does not yet demonstrate impacts on yields, income or food security.

  • More crops and plotsRules validated with agronomists and local farming organizations.
  • Richer NASA layersSoil moisture and satellite rainfall added to the climate record.
  • A community ground-truth networkSimple rain gauges and sensors that feed local measurements back into the tool.
  • Works where connection is weakA Spanish-first mobile experience designed for limited internet.

Sources and evidence

The following public sources support the regional context, the climate-data workflow and the crop-rotation rationale. They do not replace field interviews, plot-level measurements or agronomic review.

  1. 1. MiAMBIENTE – LADA: Conservación de Suelo

    Panama environmental information on land degradation and the Arco Seco, including its low annual precipitation, recurring water deficit and pressure on water resources. Use this to support the regional context, not to claim identical conditions at every farm.

  2. 2. Castrellón et al. (2021), Universidad Tecnológica de Panamá / APANAC – “Las aguas subterráneas en el Arco Seco de Panamá: Un tesoro por explorar”

    Research paper describing the Arco Seco’s location and precipitation context, and discussing groundwater pressures. The paper is regional background, not a current measurement for a particular farm.

  3. 3. Instituto de Meteorología e Hidrología de Panamá (IMHPA) – Régimen pluviométrico de Panamá

    Official description of the Pacific rainy season (May to November), the dry season (December to April), and regional rainfall patterns. These are national/regional seasonal patterns, not a forecast for a specific farm.

  4. 4. Universidad de Panamá repository – study containing Agua Buena meteorological data (table citing IDIAP, 2017)

    Reports precipitation totals for Agua Buena, Los Santos: 464.1 mm in 2015 and 1,793 mm in 2016, with a two-year mean of 1,128.6 mm/year. The table also lists temperature and humidity. This is a short historical reference, not current monitoring or a five-year NASA series.

  5. 5. NASA POWER – Daily API documentation

    Official documentation for retrieving daily time-series meteorological and solar data. Use it to define the API workflow and record the parameter names, units, dates and product selected.

  6. 6. NASA POWER – Methodology

    Explains the data sources and processing behind POWER. NASA describes meteorological inputs including MERRA-2 and GEOS, as well as higher-resolution products for selected variables. This matters when explaining what a coordinate-based result does—and does not—represent.

  7. 7. NASA POWER – Validation data sources

    Describes the in-situ datasets used for validation and their coverage. It supports transparent discussion of uncertainty; it does not establish that this prototype has been validated against a local Azuero weather station.

  8. 8. INEC Panamá – VIII Censo Nacional Agropecuario 2024, Volumen I

    Official agricultural census tables, including agricultural holdings and producers by province. Consult the relevant Herrera and Los Santos tables before adding local producer counts or farm-size statistics.

  9. 9. FAO – Crop planning and management in organic agriculture (2020)

    Technical guidance describing potential crop-rotation benefits for soil structure, fertility, weeds and pests. It supports the general rationale for considering rotations, not a specific crop sequence for an individual Azuero plot.

  10. 10. FAO GIEWS – Panama Country Brief

    Country-level food-security and crop-condition updates. Its dated reporting can provide current context, but any claim must identify the publication date and the affected provinces or crops actually named in the report.

Evidence boundary: Public sources establish regional and technical context. The team must still generate its own reproducible NASA POWER query, collect farmer testimony with informed consent, and obtain agronomic review. No interview quotation, field result, crop recommendation or measured impact should be added until it exists and can be documented.
